## Concrete source correctness / fairness examples

**REARRANGING-001 / Q81** has \(y=2(x-3)/(2x+5)\). Rearrangement yields \(x=-(5y+6)/(2(y-1))\). The original relation excludes \(x=-5/2\) and its range excludes \(y=1\); to teach inverse relations fairly, annotate these domain restrictions rather than presenting cross-multiplication as universally reversible.

**REARRANGING-020 / Q100** is \(1/y=3/(4x+3)+2\). Correct expression is \(x=-3(3y-1)/[4(2y-1)]\). An inverse form cannot allow \(y=0\) or \(y=1/2\); Q100's original wrong option C had no demonstrated single-step algebra-error mechanism in the previous ten-sample distractor QA.

**REARRANGING-023 / Q103** is \(y=1+(4x+5)/(x+6)\). Solving gives \(x=(11-6y)/(y-5)\) with original \(x\neq-6\), inverse \(y\neq5\). The original worked line `(1z-4)x` is editorially poor; the **separate NOT LIVE candidate** corrects it to `(z-4)x`. Original A/C/E/F distractors were not justified and a proposal to replace them with independently traceable wrong algebra exists in staging, but the canonical answer D must stay unchanged until independent QA approval.

**REARRANGING-025..028 / Q105–108** contain square-root reciprocal rearrangements and source-side assumptions such as \(y>1\) or \(y>2\). The constraints are material, not optional; a student should not be penalised for giving an inverse with mathematically correct explicit domain restrictions.
