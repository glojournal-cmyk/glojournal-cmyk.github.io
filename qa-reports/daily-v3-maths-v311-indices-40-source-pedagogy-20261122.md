## Independent maths check formulas applied in this focused pass
1. Q1–4: convert 4 and 8 into powers of 2, which directly gives n=2,3,4,5 for the four stated exponents; the original parameter values and x/y-independent exponent conditions were checked against the answer booklet.
2. Q5–8: \((4^a 8^{-b})^2/2^{-c}=2^{4a-6b+c}\), where \((a,b,c)=(2,1,2),(3,2,3),(4,3,4),(5,4,5)\), yielding 16, 8, 4, 2. Negative-sign-ignored error produces the printed huge distractors in all four.
3. Q9–12: \((x^a y^{-(a+1)})^2/(x^{-a}y^a)=x^{3a}y^{-(3a+2)}\), with x=2 and y=4, so \(2^{-3a-4}\) for a=1..4, giving the four source results.
4. Q13–16: independently match x and y exponents of \((x^a y^{a+1})^n/(x^{a-1}y^b)\) against both right-hand powers. All yield n=2,3,4,5.
5. Q17–20: \(2^{2n}+2^{2n+2}=5\cdot2^{2n}\). RHS 80, 320, 1280, 5120 gives n=2,3,4,5.
6. Q21–24: independently equate \(2n-p\) and \(-3n-q\) to each target exponent, obtaining n=2,3,4,5.
7. Q25–28: \(A/B=2^{n-3}\), with required exponents −1,0,1,2, so n=2,3,4,5 and \(n^2-2n=0,3,8,15\).
8. Q29–32: radical-power quotient simplifies to \(2^{2n+5}\), RHS exponents 11,13,15,17 give n=3,4,5,6.
9. Q33–36: \((x^{1/2}y^{-1/3})^{6n}/(x^p y^q)=x^{3n-p}y^{-2n-q}\); match both exponents: n=2,3,4,5. **Taught-subskill unverified**.
10. Q37–40: same radical-power quotient \(2^{2n+5}\) and RHS exponents 9,11,13,15 gives n=2,3,4,5; \(n^2-n=2,6,12,20\).
