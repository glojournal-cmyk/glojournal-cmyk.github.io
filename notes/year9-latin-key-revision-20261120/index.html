01 Decode the sentence

Endings reveal who does what.

MUST KNOW — the six cases

CaseFunctionExample
Nominativesubjectpuella = the girl
Vocativedirect addressdomine! = master!
Accusativedirect objectpuellam = the girl
Genitivepossessiondomini = of the master
Dativerecipientpuellae = to the girl
Ablativeused after certain prepositionscum servo = with the slave

These examples show one possible use. Context decides ambiguous forms.

WORKED EXAMPLE

servi epistulam dominae dabant.

  1. dabant: imperfect, third-person plural — were giving.
  2. servi: nominative plural — the slaves.
  3. epistulam: accusative singular — a letter.
  4. dominae: dative singular — to the mistress.

Translation: The slaves were giving a letter to the mistress.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

Word order alone does not identify the subject.

servum puella salutat. = The girl greets the slave.

puellam servus salutat. = The slave greets the girl.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: pueri dominum vident.

Answer: The boys see the master.

FINAL CHECK: subject • verb • object • other cases • small words

03 Adjectives must agree

Match gender, number and case.

MUST KNOW

  • An adjective agrees with its noun in THREE ways: gender, number, case. Matching letters alone is not enough.
  • Model adjective: bonus, bona, bonum = good.

WORKED EXAMPLES

  • nauta bonus = the good sailor. Nauta is masculine, although it belongs to the first declension.
  • puella servos bonos videt. = The girl sees the good slaves. servos bonos: masculine, accusative, plural.

SPECIAL FORMS

  • miser, misera, miserum = unhappy. Keep e.
  • pulcher, pulchra, pulchrum = beautiful. Use pulchr- except masculine nominative/vocative singular pulcher.

QUICK CHECK

‘Beautiful temples’ as an accusative object?

Answer: templa pulchra.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

bonorum, not bonorun.

04 Present tense

Who is doing the action now?

MUST KNOW

  • Present can mean ‘does’ or ‘is doing’.
  • portat = he/she carries OR is carrying.
Personportomoneotrahoaudio
Iportomoneotrahoaudio
you singularportasmonestrahisaudis
he/she/itportatmonettrahitaudit
weportamusmonemustrahimusaudimus
you pluralportatismonetistrahitisauditis
theyportantmonenttrahuntaudiunt

Verb meanings: carry • warn • drag • hear.

INFINITIVES HELP IDENTIFY THE CONJUGATION

  • 1st: portare
  • 2nd: monere (long e before -re)
  • 3rd: trahere (short e before -re)
  • 4th: audire

Vowel-length marks are omitted in these tables.

WORKED EXAMPLE

puellae libros legunt.

The girls read / are reading books.

legunt is third-person plural; libros is accusative plural.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

  • audio → audiunt, not audunt.
  • traho → trahunt, not trahent.
  • The ending already identifies ‘we’ or ‘you’; a subject pronoun is usually unnecessary.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: puellam monetis.

Answer: You (plural) warn / are warning the girl.

05 Imperfect tense

Ongoing or repeated action in the past.

MUST KNOW

  • Usually ‘was/were doing’ or ‘used to do’.
  • A natural English simple past may also be correct in context.
Personportomoneotrahoaudio

BUILD THE FORM

porta- + bam → portabam

mone- + bam → monebam

trahe- + bam → trahebam

audie- + bam → audiebam

WORKED EXAMPLE

ancillae aquam portabant.

The slave-women were carrying water.

  • ancillae: subject, plural.
  • aquam: object, singular.
  • portabant: imperfect, third-person plural.

BACKGROUND AND EVENT

servi laborabant. dominus advenit.

The slaves were working. The master arrived.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

  • ‘Were carrying’ is one Latin verb: portabant.
  • Do not add erant to portabant.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: in horto sedebatis.

Answer: You (plural) were sitting in the garden.

06 Perfect tense

Completed actions and changed stems.

MUST KNOW

  • Perfect commonly means ‘did’ or ‘has/have done’.
  • Use the perfect stem, not the present stem.

WORKED EXAMPLE

pueri epistulam legerunt.

The boys read the letter.

  • legerunt = perfect, third-person plural.
  • Compare: legebant = they were reading.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

  • misit = sent; mittit = sends.
  • Without vowel-length marks, advenit can be present or perfect. Use the context.

QUICK CHECK

traxerunt?

Answer: They dragged; perfect, third-person plural.

07 Be able to translate

sum, possum and infinitives.

MUST KNOW

Personsum presentsum imperfectpossum presentpossum imperfect
Isumerampossumpoteram
you singulareseraspotespoteras
he/she/itesteratpotestpoterat
wesumuseramuspossumuspoteramus
you pluralestiseratispotestispoteratis
theysunterantpossuntpoterant

sum = be. possum = be able.

Infinitives: esse = to be; posse = to be able.

BUILD THE MEANING

  • non possum + legere = I cannot read.
  • non poterant + invenire = they could not find.
  • debemus + discedere = we must / ought to leave.
  • The infinitive does not change for person or number.
  • The forms taught here are present active infinitives; Latin also has other infinitive tenses.

WORKED EXAMPLE

pueri nuntium invenire non poterant.

The boys could not find the messenger.

pueri: subject. nuntium: object. invenire: to find. non poterant: could not.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

  • potest = he/she can.
  • poterat = he/she could.
  • Do not lose non: it reverses the meaning.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: servi in villa erant.

Answer: The slaves were in the house.

08 Place and duration

Learn the case with the construction.

PREPOSITIONS

AccusativeMeaning
adto / towards
perthrough / along
circumaround
contraagainst
interbetween / among
ininto / onto
AblativeMeaning
a / abfrom / away from
e / exout of / from
cumwith
dedown from / about
inin / on

THE KEY CONTRAST

in templum = into the temple: destination.

in templo = in the temple: location.

e templo = out of the temple: ablative.

Movement alone does NOT decide the case.

ROME

Romam = to Rome.

Roma = from Rome.

Romae = at / in Rome.

These basic city constructions omit a preposition. Names of small islands can follow the same rule. The ordinary noun insula uses normal prepositions.

DURATION — HOW LONG?

quinque horas = for five hours.

decem annos = for ten years.

Accusative, without a Latin preposition.

WORKED EXAMPLE

nuntius quinque horas ambulavit.

The messenger walked for five hours.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: servi dona in villam portaverunt.

Answer: The slaves carried gifts into the house.

09 Commands and direct address

Read the verb and the person being addressed.

MUST KNOW — commands

VerbOne personSeveral people

warnmone!monete!

leadduc!ducite!

The singular command duc is irregular.

DIRECT ADDRESS — VOCATIVE

NominativeVocative singular
servusserve!
dominusdomine!
amicusamice!
puerpuer!
puellapuella!

The vocative plural normally matches the nominative plural. Do not apply a universal ‘-us becomes -i’ rule to every noun.

WORKED EXAMPLES

domine, epistulam lege!

Master, read the letter!

pueri, templum custodite!

Boys, guard the temple!

custodite addresses several people. Natural English ‘Guard!’ is sufficient.

WORDS IN SPEECH

inquit = he/she says or said in a narrative.

inquiunt = they say; historical present may be rendered ‘they said’.

AVOID THIS MISTAKE

A word inside quotation marks is not automatically vocative.

Troiani navigare debent. = The Trojans must sail.

Troiani is the subject of debent here.

QUICK CHECK

Translate: amice, in horto mane!

Answer: Friend, stay in the garden!

10 Turn knowledge into marks

Translate precisely, then write natural English.

COMPOSITION ROUTINE

  1. Identify subject, person and number.
  2. Choose the correct tense.
  3. Give nouns the correct cases.
  4. Check prepositions, non and infinitives.
  5. Read the whole sentence again.

MODEL

The messenger was giving a letter to the boys.

nuntius epistulam pueris dabat.

nuntius: subject • epistulam: object • pueris: recipient • dabat: imperfect

TRANSLATION ROUTINE

  • Find each finite verb. Match its subject.
  • Identify objects, possessors and recipients.
  • Include adjectives, negation, places and time.
  • Never guess grammatical roles from word order alone.

WORDS THAT CHANGE THE MESSAGE

LatinMeaning
nonnot
sedbut
etand
subitosuddenly
tandemat last
deindethen
diufor a long time
saepeoften (extension word)

Do not translate saepe as ‘always’; semper means ‘always’.

MINI CHALLENGE

puella epistulam domini pueris dedit, sed pueri verba legere non poterant.

Answer: The girl gave the master's letter to the boys, but the boys could not read the words.

CHECK: domini = of the master; pueris = to the boys; dedit = gave; non poterant = could not.

FINAL SELF-CHECK

  • Can I explain each ending without looking?
  • Can I translate a new sentence without help?
